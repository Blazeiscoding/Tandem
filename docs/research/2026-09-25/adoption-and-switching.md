# Adoption, switching and the cost of keeping a group together

Research date: **2026-09-25**. This memo adds to the [decision report](../../PRODUCT-RESEARCH-2026-09-25.md), [workplace research](../2026-09-24/workplace-research.md) and [roadmap](../../PRODUCT-ROADMAP-2026-09-24.md). It investigates adoption conditions that a feature comparison misses: discounted incumbents, inherited accounts and archives, gradual cutover, volunteer labor, semester changes and leadership succession.

**The strongest new conclusion is that Gatherline should prove continuity across people and tools, as well as continuity across devices.** A group can successfully install a new chat app and still fail to adopt it because the answerer stays elsewhere, the next organizer cannot operate it, old attachments remain dependent on the previous vendor, or nobody knows which channel carries essential announcements.

This is desk research, not a new runtime audit, migration benchmark, interview study or measurement of Gatherline adoption. The 21 sources below include official documentation, an independent regional survey, first-hand project discussions, institutional guidance, a conference abstract and a vendor-selected customer story. The Slack export and Zulip import pages revisit the previous combined source ledger. Source count is not an independence or quality measure. Product implications, proposed thresholds and scenarios are explicitly judgments to test. The Google Chat import boundary below follows its directly cited developer guide, not a migration test.

### A practical ownership worksheet

Use two separate outputs: **cash outlay** and **human time**. A volunteer hour is not necessarily an expense, but treating it as zero hides who pays for the system through their evenings.

| Component                         | Measurement to collect                                                                                    | Common mistake to avoid                                                              |
| --------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Incumbent expense that disappears | Actual invoice lines and contract commitments removable after cutover                                     | Counting an already-needed office suite as an entirely avoidable chat cost           |
| Setup and migration               | Export wait, administrator work, archive validation, training and integration recreation                  | Treating an upload's elapsed duration as the entire migration effort                 |
| Recurring operation               | Compute/power, connectivity/domain, storage, backup copies, media traffic and required services           | Assuming the host's existing laptop is continuously available at no operational cost |
| Support and maintenance           | Hands-on minutes for account recovery, updates, restoring files and participant questions                 | Assigning every task to the founding enthusiast indefinitely                         |
| Temporary coexistence             | Months of duplicate subscriptions, answer copying and monitoring both tools                               | Calling the switch complete while people still depend on the old workspace           |
| Exit and succession               | Export readability, fresh restoration, transfer of host/provider control and successor time               | Demonstrating recovery only with the original developer present                      |
| Outcome value                     | Missed handoffs, repeated questions, failed file retrieval and time coordinating one actual event/project | Counting messages, notification opens or screen time as benefit                      |

For a chosen planning horizon, compare:

`incremental cash = setup + migration + coexistence + recurring operation + paid support − incumbent charges actually avoided`

Record time alongside this amount by role. If the organization chooses to assign an hourly value, show that assumption and a sensitivity range rather than hiding it inside a single “savings” number. When avoided incumbent expense is zero, Gatherline must justify positive incremental cost through a demonstrated benefit or a control requirement the group values. This worksheet is a proposed evaluation method, not a measured cost model.

## 2. Migration is a set of separate promises

“Import from Slack” can mean very different things. The following comparison concerns documented scope, not independently verified execution or a ranking.

| Destination / source          | Useful documented behavior                                                                                                                                                                | Material boundary                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| ----------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Microsoft Teams               | Its current migration tool imports public/private Slack channels, messages, threads, standard reactions, files and memberships. Canvas/list content becomes HTML/JSON attachments.        | DMs, group chats, Slack Connect, workflows and custom integrations are excluded. Incremental passes omit new replies to previously imported roots and do not synchronize prior-message edits/deletions. Archived source channels need unarchiving. [A07](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoftteams/slack-to-teams-migration-tool)            |
| Zulip                         | The importer includes messages, attachments, reactions, users, channel subscriptions and custom emoji; the guide includes user/emoji API permissions and first-login setup.               | Import is into a new organization, not a merge into an established one. Preserving users does not transport passwords or eliminate authentication setup. [A08](https://zulip.com/help/import-from-slack)                                                                                                                                            |
| Mattermost                    | Documents validation, transformation, import, author/time preservation and duplicate-safe repeat import. Recommends rehearsals and fresh destinations.                                    | Thread relationships can differ; apps, canvases, starred conversations and some profile data do not migrate. The guide explicitly addresses imported unread state and missing email identities. These are vendor-documented behaviors, not benchmarked fidelity. [A09](https://docs.mattermost.com/administration-guide/onboard/migrate-from-slack) |
| Pumble                        | Supports channel messages, DMs and users when present in the input, with existing-user matching and an import history view.                                                               | Documents 250,000 messages, 2,000 users and a 200 MB ZIP limit. Slack file binaries are not imported; links remain. Imported users are invited accounts, with no automatic invitation email. [A10](https://pumble.com/help/getting-started/onboarding-for-new-users/import-data-from-slack-to-pumble/)                                              |
| Slack export itself           | Ordinary Free/Pro exports cover public-channel messages and file links; broader conversation exports have plan/approval conditions. Free-plan file links are limited to the last 90 days. | Source availability constrains every destination. An importer cannot reconstruct missing private conversations, deleted objects or unavailable attachments. [A11](https://slack.com/help/articles/201658943-Export-your-workspace-data)                                                                                                             |
| Discord personal data package | Contains the requesting person's sent messages and attachment links, plus account/server information. The page was updated September 16, 2026.                                            | It is not a complete transcript of everyone in a server. A community administrator's personal download is not a full community migration archive. [A13](https://support.discord.com/hc/en-us/articles/360004957991-Your-Discord-Data-Package)                                                                                                       |

**Source conflict retained:** Pumble's guide also says all Slack Free messages older than 90 days are unavailable for export. Slack's own export guide specifically applies its 90-day note to file links. This pass does not treat Pumble's broader assertion as established Slack behavior. Inspect a legitimately obtained pilot export and use Slack's own documentation to resolve actual source coverage. A source vendor's plan/export contract takes precedence over a destination's simplified description.

Google Chat provides another useful design precedent: it has a separate import mode with no message or mention notifications, validation/reconciliation before completion, and historical membership handling. Completion exits import mode; a space left in import mode beyond 90 days is deleted. The September 3, 2026 guide supports `SPACE` and `GROUP_CHAT`, excludes `DIRECT_MESSAGE`, and instructs importing two-person conversations into a two-member space or group chat. This does not establish conversion of that destination into a native DM. **Design inference:** Gatherline should visibly separate staging, validation and activation, while choosing its own explicit retention policy for unfinished imports. It should not silently adopt another product's deletion deadline. [A12](https://developers.google.com/workspace/chat/import-data)

### A bounded first importer contract

The existing roadmap's new-workspace boundary is reinforced by these sources. Make the result reviewable before opening it to participants. Proposed contract:

1. **Inspect first.** Show the archive type, source workspace identity, time coverage, object counts, approximate disk requirements and unavailable categories before any live membership or notifications change.
2. **Keep a source-to-destination map.** Store stable source identifiers separately from display names. Resolve duplicate names, renamed channels and authors who no longer have accounts. Preserve historical authorship without pretending the person has joined Gatherline.
3. **Separate an attachment from its URL.** Record whether each file was copied and verified, remains an external link, failed to download or was excluded. A thumbnail or working source URL on migration day does not establish independent preservation.
4. **Define identity claiming.** Importing an email string is not proof that the current claimant is its historical owner. Handle unavailable/changed addresses and guest identities without exposing historical private conversations through a convenience match.
5. **Import quietly.** Historical mentions must not ring phones, send welcome messages, execute bot actions or produce years of misleading unread badges. Define the initial reading boundary separately from archived history.
6. **Reconcile meaning, not just rows.** Count roots/replies, inspect thread context, test original attachment names and references, and preserve timestamps/time zones. Track unsupported rich objects as visible exceptions.
7. **Make resume behavior explicit.** A retry must not duplicate content. A later export containing edits, deletes or replies to old threads is a separate delta problem. Initially reject unsupported merge/delta semantics clearly rather than labeling them “sync.”
8. **Activate only after review.** Publish a coverage report and supported login instructions. Retain the source archive under the group's chosen controls. Rehearsal cancellation should clean up the staged destination without affecting the source.

These are proposed requirements, not claims that Gatherline currently implements them. Start with one real, permissioned pilot archive and a synthetic edge-case fixture; do not build every competitor importer before any group has shown a need.

### Minimum fidelity fixture for that pilot

The fixture should be small enough for human inspection and contain:

| Case                                                                 | What a passing result demonstrates                                                     |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Same display name, different people; renamed user; departed author   | Attribution is stable; live access is granted only to the correct current participant  |
| Public and private channel with overlapping members                  | Imported visibility matches the reviewed destination policy                            |
| Parent message, replies and a reply also broadcast to the channel    | Thread structure is understandable and reconciliation explains intentional duplicates  |
| Reply added to an old root after the first export                    | Unsupported delta behavior is detected or the reply is imported correctly              |
| Edited and deleted source message between passes                     | A repeat job does not silently claim synchronization it has not performed              |
| Mention of a departed user and a historical group mention            | No contact or notification is generated solely by importing history                    |
| Attachment with unavailable URL; duplicate filenames; non-Latin name | Status, checksum where copied, and retrieval by name remain understandable             |
| Channel link and message link embedded in an answer                  | The reader reaches the destination context or sees an explicit source/archive fallback |
| Unsupported canvas, list or interactive bot result                   | The importer describes the downgrade or exclusion without discarding it invisibly      |
| Canceled/restarted import and account claiming from two devices      | Duplicate safety and identity control hold across recovery paths                       |

Do not infer compatibility from one successful small export. Record source edition, export method, date, importer version, fixture coverage, observed failures and manual adjustments. The planned import adapter's support envelope should be narrower than the full set of possible Slack exports until tested.

## 3. Switching requires a group commitment and an announcement policy

Python's November 2025 public discussion supplies unusually useful counterevidence to an open-source feature argument. Thomas Wouters described a Zulip experiment that had languished while Discord became active. Antoine Pitrou disputed a simple product explanation, pointing to different timing and demand. Brett Cannon observed that another move would require someone to do the work. This is a contextual, retrospective discussion among maintainers; it cannot establish that Discord is generally better or that Zulip's current interface fails. It does show why **an enthusiastic buyer and an engaged group are different observations**. [A14](https://discuss.python.org/t/steering-council-updates/104956)

A separate November 2024 Python discussion records contributors missing the nomination period after announcement practices changed. Participants disagreed about where essential notifications should arrive, and an administrator called for an established expected route. This is a reported coordination failure, not a measured notification-delivery defect. Better push infrastructure alone would not settle the social expectation. [A15](https://discuss.python.org/t/expectations-responsibilities-around-notifications-announcements-for-core-developers/72247)

Zulip's official transition guide already covers communication policy, an introduction explaining the reason for change, a timeline and user support, in addition to technical migration. Treat this as competitive parity in adoption support. Gatherline can make those choices easier to carry out, but cannot claim that incumbent alternatives ignore transition work. [A21](https://zulip.com/help/moving-from-slack)

**Proposed adoption unit:** one complete recurring workflow with its actual participants, responsible organizer and fallback. A project lead trying Gatherline alone is an evaluator, not a retained team.

For each candidate group, make a short agreement visible in its starting conversation:

- What activity moves here, and from which date?
- Where do essential announcements arrive, how soon are people expected to check them, and what happens if somebody cannot use this app?
- Which conversations and partners remain elsewhere?
- Who answers joining/account questions, and who can step in for that person?
- What result would make the group keep Gatherline, stop the trial, or change the scope?

The agreement should be optional guidance for informal groups rather than forced administration during sign-up. Its purpose is to surface mismatched expectations before a missed event or obligation.

### Coexistence without two permanent queues

A reversible pilot needs an end condition. Proposed sequence:

1. Inventory one active workflow, its participants, current archive, necessary files and external dependencies.
2. Establish Gatherline as the primary place for that bounded workflow during the trial. Keep the old location accessible as the agreed fallback and historical reference.
3. Pin a short transition notice in both places through the organizer's normal process. This research did not contact participants or publish notices.
4. Record each return to the old tool with its reason: missing person, missing file, integration, reliability, habit, access rule or simple preference.
5. After a complete event/project cycle, choose continuation, rollback or another bounded trial. Do not expand solely because several people sent test messages.

Avoid building a bidirectional bridge as the default remedy. It introduces a separate promise about edits, deletes, identity, permissions, duplicate delivery and moderation. A manual reference link or one-way notification may be enough for one pilot. If a named group requires a bridge, give it an explicit boundary and failure policy.

## 4. The overlooked workflow is handing the group to someone else

Tufts's official student-organization transition checklist includes member/vendor contacts, historical resources, email and website accounts, social roles and e-list access; its organization system also has an explicit officer-admin transition process. This is institutional guidance, not a frequency estimate or proof of student demand for Gatherline. It makes a concrete lifecycle visible: **the organization persists while its officers change**. [A16](https://students.tufts.edu/office-campus-life/student-organizations/leadership-transition)

Zulip's classroom guide distinguishes separate class organizations from shared departmental spaces and gives end-of-class steps: rename channels for the term, restrict old solutions, optionally deactivate former students, or export/archive an organization. This shows that “keep all history” and “let the next cohort see all history” are different requirements. Treat the guide as a workflow precedent; no classroom was observed in this pass. [A18](https://zulip.com/help/setting-up-zulip-for-a-class)

The UCSD customer story describes Kiran Kedlaya's pandemic-era teaching across time zones, with asynchronous Q&A, lecture files, mathematical notation, answer hiding and local-time event display. It is vendor-selected testimony about 2020–21 use, not evidence of current adoption rates or a controlled comparison. It usefully identifies a task bundle in which “students” means more than casual group chat. [A17](https://zulip.com/case-studies/ucsd/)

### Cohort-specific trial additions

| Cohort                          | Additional complete task                                                                       | What to observe                                                                                                                        |
| ------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Student project team            | Replace the outgoing coordinator, invite a new collaborator and find the prior submission file | Authority transfer, correct history scope, changed institutional email and ability to continue without the departing person            |
| Class or workshop               | Finish one cohort, keep instructor material, start the next and invite a new assistant         | Old answers remain appropriately scoped; a fresh participant is not dropped into obsolete announcements                                |
| Club                            | Incoming secretary runs the next event using last year's contacts, schedule and files          | The successor can discover context, manage participants and cancel/reschedule an event                                                 |
| Nonprofit staff plus volunteers | Add seasonal volunteers to one activity, announce a shift change and remove access afterwards  | Staff/volunteer roles match actual work; occasional participants receive the agreed information without joining every internal channel |
| Small organization              | A second administrator operates the service while the first is unavailable                     | Provider/domain access, backups, account recovery and notification service ownership are understandable                                |
| Friends/family                  | Another trusted person becomes the host after a device change                                  | Hosting responsibility can change without implying that all participants become administrators or exposing private conversations       |

**Proposed successor drill:** the original operator supplies a documented handover, then observes without helping while a second person finds the host, checks backup freshness, restores an isolated copy, identifies the live address and handles one account request. This is stricter than proving that the same developer can restore their own installation. Measure assistance and missing knowledge before adding a polished wizard.

Keep three permissions distinct: group membership administration, server/provider operation and access to historical content. A product handover should not normalize passing around a personal password. The complete design must account for recovery when the only owner is unavailable, while preventing ordinary members from taking over a workspace by assertion. No automatic succession policy is selected by this research.

## 5. Ownership can mean reduced administration, not just self-hosting

Berkeley CLTC's April 2026 CyberCAN Washington report surveyed **100 nonprofits** and interviewed three nonprofits with major incidents plus eight local-government personnel. It reports that **64% had no full-time IT staff** and **65% had someone handling IT/security outside their main job**. The sample came from 21 Washington counties and skewed toward smaller organizations. These are regional, self-reported descriptive results; recruitment representativeness and causal effects of different hosting choices are not established here. The study is about organizational capacity, not preference for chat products. It supports measuring the burden transferred to a volunteer before treating self-hosting as an automatic benefit. [A20, methodology pp. 9–10 and staffing pp. 22–23](https://cltc.berkeley.edu/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/CyberCAN-Washington.pdf)

DESY's March 18, 2026 CS3 contribution describes a transition from Mattermost to Nextcloud Talk to combine messaging, file sharing and collaborative editing within existing infrastructure and its digital-sovereignty strategy. This is a first-hand institutional abstract, not an independently evaluated outcome. Its slides could not be retrieved by the research browser; no timing, savings or migration-completeness claim is taken from the slides' search snippets. **Inference:** another open-source chat product may lose to an existing open-source suite because consolidation is valuable. Gatherline should test linking to tools a group already uses before assuming it needs its own documents/boards suite. [A19](https://indico.cern.ch/event/1560960/contributions/6914984/)

This creates three distinct buying situations worth keeping separate:

| Situation                                        | Likely evaluation question — hypothesis                                                          | Suitable first evidence                                                                    |
| ------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Group already has a trusted operator             | Can we keep our conversations usable under our chosen hosting arrangement?                       | Join/return plus restore, export and successor drill                                       |
| Group has institutional IT and an existing suite | Does this replace a concrete coordination problem without multiplying support and identity work? | One guest/integration workflow and an administrator's actual support inventory             |
| Group has no willing operator                    | Can somebody provide a dependable service at an acceptable total cost?                           | An assisted-deployment/service prototype with named responsibilities and real support time |

The third situation does not automatically justify building a hosted billing platform. First determine who will run a pilot and what they must do when a participant loses access or the service stops. A supported installation recipe, trusted community host or managed pilot can test this question before a commercial service is designed.

## 7. Research protocol that can reject the preferred story

Add these sessions to the existing multi-cohort pilot plan rather than increasing feature scope immediately. All tasks below are proposed; none was run during this desk-research pass.

### Interview the organizer, successor and reluctant participant separately

Use a 30–45 minute session anchored to recent events. Do not ask only whether they like a self-hosted Slack alternative.

1. “Show the last time somebody joined late or could not find the correct conversation.” Capture the route and assistance, without collecting private message content unnecessarily.
2. “What happens when the person who runs this leaves or is unavailable?” Ask for the actual last handover, account contacts and who can restore service.
3. “Which people or systems would prevent you closing the old app?” Request a concrete example of each dependency.
4. “Show a file or answer you would need from six months ago.” Determine whether the useful object is a chat message, attachment, document or external issue.
5. “How did the last important announcement reach occasional members?” Compare the sender's expectation with the reluctant participant's account.
6. “What are you paying now, what discount have you actually received, and which expense would disappear?” Permit ranges; do not require bills containing personal or financial details.
7. “Tell us about the last tool the group tried and abandoned.” Record reasons even when unrelated to interface quality.

Keep the organizer's account separate from other members' responses. A champion may value data control while other participants value existing contacts or avoiding another installation. That disagreement is product evidence, not a nuisance to filter out.

### Task trials

| Trial                  | Setup                                                                        | Evidence to record                                                                       | Stop/revise signal                                                                              |
| ---------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Migration feasibility  | Synthetic fixture plus one authorized, minimized archive                     | Source coverage, unavailable objects, manual interventions, wall-clock and hands-on time | Essential history is absent at source, access expands incorrectly, or reconciliation is unclear |
| Independent retrieval  | Disconnect the test client's source-vendor session; use a safe isolated copy | Which files/messages actually remain usable, including original context                  | “Imported” content still depends on unplanned source authentication or links                    |
| Group activation       | One scheduled project/event cycle, with all essential roles represented      | Join failures, unanswered requests, repeated old-tool use and organizer assistance       | Work requires parallel attendance in both apps for reasons the pilot cannot resolve             |
| Announcement agreement | One routine change and one time-sensitive but non-emergency update           | Who received, understood and acted within the agreed interval; delivery channel          | Sender and participant expectations differ, even if technical delivery succeeds                 |
| Successor operation    | Original operator silent; second operator follows handover                   | Recovery, provider/account understanding, unresolved dependencies                        | Recovery works only with undocumented knowledge from the founder                                |
| Cohort rollover        | Synthetic completed class/club season and new participants                   | History visibility, role changes, useful carryover and obsolete notification state       | New people see material they should not, or reusable information becomes inaccessible           |

Use synthetic or explicitly permissioned records. Record task outcomes and exception categories rather than copying whole conversations into the research log. An archive access review is part of the test setup, not a license to collect participants' unrelated private history.

### Suggested decisions after the first complete cycle

- **Continue:** essential participants voluntarily return for the agreed workflow, the operator can explain the responsibilities, and no unresolved access or integrity failure remains.
- **Narrow scope:** Gatherline helps one activity but the group needs existing partner/institutional tools. Keep a complementary use case instead of forcing whole-organization replacement.
- **Change delivery model:** participants value the workflow but no one can operate the host. Test assisted operation before adding more messaging features.
- **Defer migration:** required history is unavailable or the import support envelope cannot cover it. Keep native export independently useful; do not conceal the gap with a success banner.
- **Reject the hypothesis:** participants prefer their incumbent after a fair complete-task comparison, or the new tool adds coordination labor without a benefit they can name.

### Unknowns that still matter

No public source here establishes Gatherline's support workload, willingness to pay, group conversion rate, import throughput, archive-size distribution or probability of operator turnover. No Indian club/nonprofit procurement or eligibility claim is established from the U.S.-weighted institutional evidence. Interviews should include locally relevant groups, phone-only participants, limited bandwidth and people with no institutional email.
