# Personal and community communication research

**Research period: September 24–25, 2026.** This supplement examines adoption beyond workplace chat: friends, families, clubs, creators, students and public communities. It supports the user's **“for everyone”** direction through a shared core with optional capabilities. The eighteen registered internet sources were accessed September 24; the additional source-code checks below were completed September 25. Filenames retain the review's start date.

Eighteen sources were read: ten official documents, three vendor announcements, four first-hand discussions and one primary research paper. [The source register](community-sources.json) records dates, claims and limitations. “Documented” below means verified against a primary product source, not independently tested in the application. User discussions identify failure modes, not their prevalence. Vendor claims about engagement or superiority are not accepted as research findings.

Gatherline comparisons use [the current-source audit](../../PRODUCT-AUDIT-2026-09-24.md): friends are server-local; responsive layouts exist, but dependable phone push, recording voice notes, polls/events, DM consent and blocking/reporting are incomplete or absent. This research proposes changes; it does not claim those changes were implemented.

## Discord: belonging requires both orientation and boundaries

**Documented advantage:** Discord onboarding can select roles and channels from a few answers, with later customization. That is more useful than presenting every newcomer with the same channel list. However, the documented setup requires seven default channels, five writable by everyone, and desktop administration. Gatherline should borrow interest selection and newcomer preview without inheriting those setup requirements. A family group with one conversation should be a valid complete setup. [C01: onboarding](https://support.discord.com/hc/en-us/articles/11074987197975-Community-Onboarding-FAQ)

**Counterevidence:** A server administrator reports that newcomers still needed help finding channels despite apparently correct permissions. This is one unresolved account, not proof of a general Discord defect. It illustrates a design trap: “allowed to access,” “joined” and “shown in my sidebar” are separate concepts that people can mistake for one another. Gatherline should explain hidden channels and let hosts preview the exact newcomer experience. [C05: administrator report](https://www.reddit.com/r/discordhelp/comments/1smhmjp/server_onboarding_help_unsolved/)

Forum channels add titled posts, tags, guidelines, searchable discussion lists and a gallery layout. These offer better retrieval for questions, showcases and recommendations than an undifferentiated stream. The cost is another conversation type and choices about tags and inactivity. Gatherline should offer an optional discussion view over familiar conversations, not demand titles for casual DMs. [C04: forums](https://support.discord.com/hc/en-us/articles/6208479917079-Forum-Channels-FAQ)

Discord also documents DM controls, blocking and friend-request permissions. Shared-server users can send DMs by default. Gatherline should make its own policy explicit: approved contacts, requests from members, or open DMs. Joining a community must not silently communicate willingness to receive personal messages. [C02: privacy controls](https://support.discord.com/hc/en-us/articles/217916488-Blocking-Privacy-Settings)

Sustainable moderation needs more than owner deletion powers. Discord's AutoMod combines filtering, alerts and timeouts, but documents English-only built-in word lists and spam filtering. Gatherline should start with rate limits, report queues, reversible actions, reasons and role separation; configurable automation follows. Measure queue age, repeat abuse and overturned actions. Avoid claiming universal language coverage or making a volunteer read every channel. These are our design recommendations, informed by the documented capabilities and limits. [C03: AutoMod](https://support.discord.com/hc/en-us/articles/4421269296535-AutoMod-FAQ)

## Discourse: give useful conversations a second life

Discourse documents chat thread titles/tracking, quoting chat into topics, moderator channel moves and configurable retention. The advantage over Gatherline's current pins and saved messages is a path from fleeting discussion to durable reference. Its documented channel retention defaults to 90 days, demonstrating that durability and deletion policy must be designed together. Copying that particular default is not the recommendation. [C06: maintained chat documentation](https://meta.discourse.org/t/discourse-chat/230881)

A historical first-hand discussion supplies valuable disagreement. Some participants found transcript quoting inadequate because authorship, reply routing and continuing discussion became awkward. Staff described deliberate summary-and-context creation as useful curation. Neither position establishes a universal answer. Gatherline should offer “Create a discussion/answer from this,” preserving source links and attribution, with an explicit destination and visibility check. Keep the source conversation intact unless someone deliberately moves it; never expose a private conversation merely by publishing a summary. [C07: discussion and counterarguments](https://meta.discourse.org/t/moving-conversations-from-chat-to-topics/253209)

## Signal: distinguish identity, discoverability and access

Signal documents separate controls for phone-number visibility and discovery, plus exact usernames, QR codes and links to initiate contact. It still requires a phone number, and does not provide a searchable username directory. The lesson is the separation of decisions, not a recommendation to require phone numbers in Gatherline. A user should know what identifier they share, who can find them, and what happens when they revoke it. [C08: usernames and privacy](https://support.signal.org/hc/en-us/articles/6712070553754-Phone-Number-Privacy-and-Usernames)

For Gatherline, a short profile/contact link and QR invitation can improve personal adoption without introducing federation. Show the host and intended recipient before account creation; land the person in the promised conversation afterwards. Explain that an account on one server is not automatically an account on another. Optional aliases or linking must not silently merge school, family and work identities.

Signal call links are reusable, revocable, can require administrator approval, and expire after 90 unused days; participants need Signal. The benefit is less setup for a recurring social call. It is not evidence of account-free browser guest access. Gatherline should eventually add direct call invitations and a simple lobby, while defining guest permissions, abuse controls and relay costs separately. [C09: call-link behavior](https://support.signal.org/hc/en-us/articles/7860719423002-How-to-create-and-share-call-links)

## WhatsApp: everyday coordination deserves priority

Meta's event announcement describes RSVPs, approaching-event notifications and findable event details. Announcement replies are grouped and muted. Its polls announcement describes single-choice voting, poll search and retaining or editing captions when forwarding media. These are concrete capabilities; the promotional claim that they strengthen communities is not independently established here. [C10: events](https://about.fb.com/news/2024/05/events-in-whatsapp-communities/), [C11: polls and media captions](https://about.fb.com/news/2023/05/whatsapp-polls-updates-sharing-with-captions/)

Gatherline should support deciding a time, sharing an album and organizing a gathering before investing heavily in decorative social features. Start with single/multiple-choice polls and event cards containing time zone, location/call link, RSVP, cancellation and optional reminders. Put them in a findable shared-items view. Polls must disclose whether votes are public, hidden from members or visible to administrators; avoid a vague “anonymous” promise.

Media captions in the WhatsApp source mean accompanying text, not speech transcription. Gatherline should preserve context during forwarding, support photo selection/captions and make video/audio playback work without a download detour. External social-media previews should be optional and fetched safely; they introduce tracking, bandwidth and moderation costs. Custom emoji can support group identity, but an algorithmic public feed is a separate product hypothesis without evidence from this review.

## Voice and captions: inclusion has costs on both sides

A CHI 2022 primary study worked with isiXhosa-speaking and Marathi-speaking communities. It documents the value of voice messaging, difficulties retrieving recordings and transcription challenges shaped by language and context. Its situated qualitative findings do not establish worldwide preferences or today's recognition accuracy. They do challenge a desktop, English, text-only definition of “everyone.” [C15: research paper](https://reitmaier.io/publications/chi2022_asr_opportunities/Reitmaier_2022_ASR-Opportunities-Challenges.pdf)

Our proposed implementation is short voice notes with preview, cancel, seek, speed control, visible duration and retry; then optional transcript generation with the original recording always available. Speech recognition needs language testing, error correction and explicit processing/storage choices. Live call captions should be evaluated separately for latency, accuracy, consent and hosting cost. They are an accessibility proposal, not a capability claimed for every competitor. Text alternatives, screen-reader controls and low-bandwidth media settings remain necessary even when transcription exists.

## Telegram: convenience must not blur privacy language

Telegram documents cloud synchronization, groups, broadcast channels and expressive media. It also clearly distinguishes cloud chats from device-specific end-to-end encrypted Secret Chats. Cloud group chats are not end-to-end encrypted. Its own claims of security superiority are vendor opinion and are not adopted. [C12: Telegram FAQ](https://telegram.org/faq)

The useful comparison is discoverable shared media, easy multi-device continuity and separate broadcast/discussion modes. Gatherline can add an announcement mode and improve galleries without copying Telegram's entire platform. Its current ownership story must accurately say who controls storage and who can read content. If encrypted private conversations are added, search, bots, backups, reports and device recovery need an explicit design. A lock icon must not imply a stronger protection than the implementation provides.

## Matrix and Element: federation does not remove identity work

Matrix documents shared rooms across homeservers, with account identifiers containing a username and server domain. This goes beyond Gatherline's separate server-local accounts, but introduces distributed identity and trust; it is not equivalent to a portable account that effortlessly changes host. Bridges also add another identity and access surface. [C13: Matrix concepts](https://matrix.org/docs/matrix-concepts/elements-of-matrix/)

Element's recovery key is distinct from the password. Its documentation explains recovery through verified devices and warns that an identity reset can lose access to encrypted history. A person inviting relatives described earlier encryption difficulties; replies also described improvements. That anecdote is a prompt to test family onboarding and recovery, not grounds to reject encryption or assert Element is currently unusable. [C14: recovery documentation](https://docs.element.io/latest/element-support/device-verification/how-to-ensure-you-have-a-recovery-key/), [C18: family-adoption discussion](https://www.reddit.com/r/elementchat/comments/1r5jq6o/encryption_and_new_nontechie_users/)

For Gatherline, settle the vocabulary and trust model now. Smooth account switching is near-term work; shared identity, federated messaging and bridging are separate later projects. Any E2EE pilot must include replacing a lost phone and understanding unrecoverable history, not only sending an encrypted message.

## Stoat: compare dated failure reports with newer delivery

In February 2026, several Stoat users reported awkward mobile navigation, messages appearing unsent, missing updates and absent notifications while trying a Discord alternative. These are credible examples of frustration, but a small self-selected thread cannot establish prevalence or present status. [C17: first-hand reports](https://github.com/orgs/stoatchat/discussions/1352)

The July 17 release explicitly announces video/screensharing, message/call sounds and push across several clients. It also acknowledges 720p30 sharing, Wayland audio trouble and granular notification controls still to come. Therefore, “Stoat has no mobile calling or push” would be stale criticism. The lesson is to measure complete journeys, not compare static feature checkmarks. [C16: later release](https://stoat.chat/release-notes/93eb1f4ec77444ca9fb13617e0e6c0b1)

## Three current-source distinctions that change priorities

These are **code-only findings checked September 25**, separate from competitor documentation and the root agent's runtime walkthrough.

**Calling a person differs from joining a room.** Gatherline's header starts or joins a channel/DM huddle; the inspected protocol exposes participants and media signaling, without a ringing invitation, accept/decline or missed-call event. A friend pressing “call Sam” reasonably needs an invitation that reaches Sam. Prioritize caller/ringing/accepted/declined/missed states, notification delivery and a visible call-back action before larger meeting capacity. Respect DND, blocks and repeated-call limits. Keep quiet drop-in huddles as a separate choice. Evidence: [HuddleBar.tsx](../../../packages/ui/src/components/HuddleBar.tsx), lines 168–204; [events.ts](../../../packages/protocol/src/events.ts), lines 63–66 and 141–143. The expectation is a product hypothesis to validate, not a population statistic.

**Quote reply differs from a thread.** The message action opens “Reply in thread.” No separate contextual quote-reply action was found there. Provide both: a lightweight quoted reply in the current conversation and a thread for a side discussion. Preserve source attribution and handle edited/deleted originals. Evidence: [MessageItem.tsx](../../../packages/ui/src/components/MessageItem.tsx), lines 241–242.

**Switching accounts differs from staying reachable in all accounts.** Opening another workspace destroys the previous client before connecting the replacement. This single active session cannot be described as simultaneous cross-workspace notifications or a unified inbox. An optional background connection manager would require separate unread, privacy, resource and reconnect policies. Evidence: [App.tsx](../../../packages/ui/src/App.tsx), lines 88–99. Evaluate that smaller investment independently of federation.

## Adoption evidence to collect next

Run moderated trials with a family/friends group, a club/community and a project group, including low-end phones, mixed languages and at least one assistive-technology user. Invite whole small groups so the test includes real interaction; do not treat isolated sign-ups as adoption.

Measure invite-to-first-reply success, unassisted return after a day, missed/duplicate notifications, finding a shared photo or decision, joining a call on another network, recovering a replaced device, and moderator time per incident. Track recurring reasons people return to their previous messenger. Set numerical targets after an initial baseline; do not invent conversion percentages from these sources.

The evidence supports a universal core that adapts to relationships and context. It does not support shipping a large public social network, mandatory phone identity, federation, an AI assistant and enterprise administration all at once.
