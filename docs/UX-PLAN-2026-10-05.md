# Tandem UI and UX improvement plan — 5 October 2026

This plan covers the two things people see: the **desktop app** and the **browser client** that every
workspace server serves at its own address (the "website" an invitee opens). Both render
`packages/ui`, so almost every item below improves both at once. A separate, optional track covers a
public **project website**, which Tandem does not have yet.

It was written against `b9da156` after using the product, not only reading it:

- Built the web client, server bundle and Electron app from this checkout.
- Walked a **fresh** workspace (owner sign-up, first run) and a **seeded** one (`scripts/seed-demo.mjs`)
  through about 60 screens: sign-in, channels, threads, hover actions, Jump to, search, Activity,
  Saved, Threads, Scheduled, Friends, Browse, New message, Invite, People, Apps, Account settings,
  status, send later and a huddle.
- Repeated the core screens at **1440×900, 768×1024 and 390×844** (touch emulation), with the device
  preferring light, and in the **Electron app** under a virtual display (join, host, Manage hosting).
- Ran **axe-core in real Chromium**, contrast included, on sign-in and the workspace in both themes.
- Read the theme, the shared components and the earlier UI history and audits, then compared the
  findings with published guidance and with how Slack, Linear, Discord, Superhuman and others solved
  the same problems. [Sources](#sources) are listed at the end.

Screenshots referenced below are in [review-assets/2026-10-05](review-assets/2026-10-05/). They

### Fifth pass: the chaicode.com look

The owner asked for the style of [ui.chaicode.com](https://ui.chaicode.com/), the shadcn registry
chaicode.com is built from. Its published description guided this pass, since the site itself was
not reachable from the build environment: dark by default, Manrope for reading, Montserrat for
titles and one pale-cream highlight phrase per paragraph, Onest for names, Geist Mono for code, warm
hairline cards with a faint fill and a little blur instead of shadows, buttons with asymmetric
corners, frosted menus with the brand colour on focus, a hexagon glow that stays warm in light
mode, and a badge with an orange glint running round its edge.

- **Tokens.** Onyx is warm near-black (`#0a0a0a` page, `#100f0e` cards) with warm greys for text;
  White is warm paper (`#f4efe6`). The accent is chaicode orange, `#f97316`, deepened to `#a3470c`
  on paper. `--color-cream` is the highlight phrase. Every text colour still clears 4.5:1 on every
  surface in both themes, and the window, page, manifest and icon colours follow.
- **Type.** The four faces replace Inter, bundled and offline like before; headings take Montserrat,
  the wordmark and workspace names Onest.
- **Surfaces.** `.card-warm` (hairline, faint fill, 4 px blur, no shadow) for cards, the Jump to
  button and empty-state marks; `.surface-float` menus are frosted; `.btn-shape` cuts primary,
  secondary and danger buttons' corners unevenly; inputs and the composer show the orange on focus.
- **Atmosphere.** `.hex-glow` behind the sign-in page, and a fainter one behind the sidebar; a
  `.glint-badge` above the sign-in headline, still for anyone who asks for less motion.

## The verdict

**Tandem is accessible and capable but loud and crowded.** The foundations are better than the
first impression suggests. The problem is hierarchy and information architecture: every element
speaks at the same volume, the accent colour is on almost everything, and nearly every feature is
visible at once. Fixing that is mostly subtraction and reorganisation, not new features.

### Keep these — they are good

- **Accessibility.** axe-core found **zero violations** on sign-in and the workspace, in Onyx and
  White, with colour contrast checked in a real browser. Focus rings, the roving message list, labelled
  dialogs and the reduced-motion handling are all right. No change below may regress this.
- **A real design system in waiting.** `theme.css` documents surfaces, a type scale and a radius
  scale, and `theme.test.ts` fails on sizes outside them. A White theme exists and reads well.
- **Honest states.** `ListStatus` gives lists loading, failed and empty states with stand-in rows,
  and sends are optimistic. The product rarely leaves someone guessing what happened.
- **Phone basics.** A drawer, a message `⋮` menu instead of hover, a full-screen thread and a folded
  formatting bar already exist.

### What is wrong, in five themes

1. **No hierarchy.** The accent is used for unread, selection, links, primary buttons, info notices,
   uppercase eyebrows ("MAKE YOURSELF AT HOME", "YOUR SHARED SPACE") and outlines — the `copper`
   token appears **230 times** in `packages/ui/src`. Add 81 `font-mono` uses and 54 underlines and
   nothing stands out, because everything does. _Refactoring UI_: de-emphasise the secondary instead
   of amplifying the primary.
2. **Too much at once.** Hovering your own message raises **14 buttons**
   ([message-toolbar.png](review-assets/2026-10-05/message-toolbar.png)). The sidebar stacks Jump to,
   a Getting started card and five navigation rows before the first channel. Search shows five filter
   controls, a syntax sheet and recent searches before its first result. Manage hosting is one long
   scroll. Hick's law: every extra option slows every decision.
3. **Inconsistent containers.** Friends is a modal, Saved and Threads are side panels, "Set a status"
   opens the full eight-section Account settings, Activity and Threads have manual **Refresh** buttons
   and **Previous page / Next page** in a real-time app. People cannot predict where something will
   open or whether it is live.
4. **The first run asks before it gives.** A new owner lands in an empty `#general` under a
   notification-permission banner, beside a checklist whose labels are cut off ("Create a cha…",
   "Turn on notificati…") ([first-run-workspace.png](review-assets/2026-10-05/first-run-workspace.png)).
   In the desktop app, hosting — the thing a first-time user most likely came to do — is a footnote
   link ([desktop-join.png](review-assets/2026-10-05/desktop-join.png)).
5. **The phone is a shrunken desktop.** All navigation is behind a hamburger, the drawer spends 72 px
   on a one-workspace rail and shows a "Ctrl K" hint, and the channel intro fills the first screen
   ([phone.png](review-assets/2026-10-05/phone.png)). NN/g measured that hiding navigation cuts its
   discoverability almost in half.

## Principles for every change

Use these to judge each pull request, and to settle arguments.

1. **The conversation is the loudest thing on screen.** Chrome, labels and navigation recede.
2. **One accent, for one meaning.** Colour marks what needs you (unread, mentions, the one primary
   action). Links, labels and decoration use ink, weight and space.
3. **Show the next step; reveal the rest on request.** Progressive disclosure, not removal.
4. **Familiar beats novel.** People arrive from Slack, Discord and WhatsApp (Jakob's law). Put things
   where they expect them, and only differ where Tandem is genuinely different (hosting).
5. **Never block; prefer undo.** No disabled submit buttons, no confirmation where an undo works.
6. **Live means live.** No Refresh buttons on data the socket already delivers.
7. **Fast is a feature.** Respond within 100 ms; never animate something triggered by the keyboard or
   used dozens of times a day.
8. **Every screen works at 390 px, with a keyboard, in both themes.**

## Phase 0 — Fix what is broken

**UX-01 · Make Send later a real popover (S).** It is a hand-rolled list with no Escape, outside-click
or focus handling, and its toggle has no `aria-expanded`
([Composer.tsx:936–1030](../packages/ui/src/components/Composer.tsx#L936)). Build it on a shared
Popover primitive (UX-14) or the existing `Menu`. _Done when:_ Escape and an outside click close it,
focus returns to the clock button, switching conversation closes it, and a composer test covers all
three.

**UX-02 · Follow the device's theme by default (S).** Default to `"system"` for devices with no saved
choice; keep saved choices as they are ([appearance.ts:31](../packages/ui/src/lib/appearance.ts#L31)).
_Done when:_ a fresh profile preferring light opens in White, and a saved "dark" stays dark.

**UX-03 · Ask for notification permission in context (S–M).** The banner is already a soft
pre-prompt — the browser's own prompt only appears after **Turn on**, and **Not now** keeps it away
for two weeks ([NotificationBanner.tsx](../packages/ui/src/components/NotificationBanner.tsx)). Its
timing is the problem: it is the first thing a new member sees, above an empty conversation, before
anyone has sent them anything. Chrome's data on web.dev: prompts shown without a user signal are
allowed **12%** of the time; after an interaction, **30%** — and a denial cannot be asked again. Show
it after the person's first sent message, or when a DM or mention arrives while the window is in the
background, and keep the Getting started step. _Done when:_ a new account sees no banner on arrival,
it appears after the first send, and the two-week snooze is unchanged.

**UX-04 · Getting started without truncation (S).** Labels are cut off and each step repeats itself
("Create a channel … New channel")
([GettingStarted.tsx:147](../packages/ui/src/components/GettingStarted.tsx#L147)). Make each step one
full-width row whose label is the action, add a thin progress bar, and collapse the card to one line
("2 of 4 done") after the first visit. _Done when:_ no label truncates in a 320 px sidebar and each row
is a single target at least 32 px tall.

**UX-05 · Copy and formatting fixes (S).** For a known workspace, head the page "Sign in to
Northwind" or "Join Northwind" and drop "All workspaces" in the browser client. "0 of 8 people
selected" becomes "Add up to 8 people". State username rules under the field ("Lowercase letters and
numbers") instead of only in the error. Separate network failures from unknown errors. Add one
`formatWhen()` helper ("8:31 PM", "Yesterday", "Mon 3 Oct", "3 Oct 2025") and use it in Activity,
search, Saved, Threads, Scheduled and devices. Show a DM's name once. _Done when:_ no panel calls
`toLocaleDateString()` directly, and a test covers today, yesterday, this week and older.

**UX-06 · Platform-aware shortcuts (S).** One `shortcutLabel()` that shows ⌘K on macOS, Ctrl K
elsewhere, and nothing on touch-only devices; use it in the sidebar, tooltips, composer hint and the
shortcuts sheet. _Done when:_ the phone drawer shows no keyboard hint and macOS shows ⌘.

**UX-07 · Stop disabling submit buttons (S–M).** Sign-in, Join workspace, Start hosting, Start
conversation and Create app are disabled until filled, so the button gives no reason
([JoinScreen.tsx:1016](../packages/ui/src/screens/JoinScreen.tsx#L1016)). Keep them enabled, validate
on submit, and put a specific message next to the field ("Enter a username", not "Invalid request").
Adam Silver and Vercel's guidelines both say the same. Disabling Send in an empty composer is the one
convention to keep. _Done when:_ every form submits and explains what is missing, and focus moves to
the first field with a problem.

**UX-08 · Real-time panels without Refresh or paging (M).** Activity, Threads, Saved and Scheduled
update from the socket; remove their Refresh buttons
([MessageListPanel.tsx:108](../packages/ui/src/components/MessageListPanel.tsx#L108),
[ActivityPanel.tsx:138](../packages/ui/src/components/ActivityPanel.tsx#L138)) and replace
Previous/Next page with loading more on scroll. Keep Retry for failures. _Done when:_ a mention sent by
another user appears in an open Activity panel without interaction.

## Phase 1 — Foundations

**UX-10 · Accent discipline (M).** Give colour semantic roles in `theme.css`: `accent` (primary action,
current selection), `attention` (unread, mentions), `link`, `success`, `danger` and `info`. Today one
token covers them all. Rules:

- At most one filled-accent control per view (the primary action).
- Links in messages use the link colour; links in chrome use ink with an underline on hover.
- Informational notices ("This workspace is brand new") use a neutral surface and an icon, not accent
  text on an accent tint.
- Remove the uppercase accent eyebrows and the channel-intro eyebrow
  ([MessageTimeline.tsx:617](../packages/ui/src/components/MessageTimeline.tsx#L617)).
- Mono is for code, addresses and invite codes; timestamps and counts use sans with tabular figures.

_Done when:_ the accent count in components falls by at least half, a `theme.test.ts` rule caps it, and
side-by-side screenshots show the unread and mention states as the only coloured text in the sidebar.

**UX-11 · Decide Tandem's own identity (M, design decision).** The accent is another product's brand
colour, the mark is an orange rounded square with a T, and the workspace tile directly below it in the
rail is also an orange rounded square — two identical tiles. Choose deliberately: either adopt the
orange as Tandem's and say so, or pick a distinct accent. Either way, build the palette as a
perceptually even scale in OKLCH (as Linear did with LCH, and as Evil Martians recommend) so both
themes and any future custom theme are generated from a few inputs. Give workspaces their own
colour or uploaded icon, and update the README description. _Done when:_ the brand and a workspace
can be told apart at a glance, and the palette is generated, not hand-picked.

**UX-12 · Bundle one interface font (S–M).** The font stack falls back to whatever the system has —
Segoe on Windows, San Francisco on macOS, often DejaVu on Linux, which renders noticeably wider — so the
tested type scale looks different on every platform. Bundle a variable sans such as Inter through
`@fontsource` (offline, like the earlier rework required; never a Google Fonts link), enable tabular
numbers for times and counts, and keep the system mono for code. _Done when:_ the web and desktop
builds render the same metrics on Windows, macOS and Linux, and the bundle-size guard still passes.

**UX-13 · Density and rhythm (S).** With the font fixed, re-check the sidebar: navigation and channel
rows both use 15 px, the same as message text, and the section labels are uppercase and spaced. Use
14 px for sidebar rows, sentence-case section labels in ink-faint, and one row height everywhere
(32 px comfortable, 28 px compact). _Done when:_ at 1440×900 the sidebar shows at least 14 rows below
the header without scrolling.

**UX-14 · Primitives everyone uses (M).** There is one `<Button>` usage beside 285 raw `<button>`
elements, two local `inputCls` definitions beside 66 raw inputs, and popovers built by hand. Finish
the set in `packages/ui/src/components`: `Button`, `IconButton` (requires a label and shows a
tooltip), `TextField` (label, hint, error), `Select`, `Popover`, `Badge`, `Kbd`, `EmptyState`,
`Sheet` (phone), alongside the existing `Modal`, `Menu`, `Tooltip`, `Toast` and `ListStatus`. Add a
development-only gallery route that renders each in both themes. _Done when:_ new UI code imports
primitives, and the gallery is the reference for review.

**UX-15 · Visual regression (M).** The e2e suite saves screenshots but never compares them
([web.spec.ts:407](../tests/e2e/web.spec.ts#L407)). Add `toHaveScreenshot` baselines for twelve key
screens in two themes at desktop and phone widths, with the seeded data and a fixed clock. Run axe
with contrast in the same pass. _Done when:_ a colour or spacing regression fails CI with a diff.

## Phase 2 — Shell and navigation

**UX-20 · A shorter sidebar (M).** Before the first channel the sidebar shows Jump to, Getting
started, and Activity, Threads, Friends, Saved and Scheduled.

- Keep **Activity** and **Threads**.
- Merge **Saved** and **Scheduled** into **Later**, with Saved and Scheduled tabs. This also ends the
  "Saved" row opening a panel titled "Later" noted in the September audit, and matches Slack's 2023
  redesign.
- Move **Friends** next to Direct messages (a people icon in that header), where it belongs.
- Add **Starred** and collapsible sections (already on the roadmap).

_Done when:_ there are at most three rows above Channels, and at 1280×720 at least six channels are
visible with Getting started open.

**UX-21 · Show the workspace rail only when it helps (S).** With one workspace the rail spends 72 px
on the brand tile, one workspace tile and a plus. Hide it until there is a second sign-in; "Add or
join a workspace" stays in the workspace menu. _Done when:_ a single-workspace user sees no rail,
desktop or phone.

**UX-22 · Menus by ownership (M).** Today the gear menu mixes workspace administration (Invite,
People, Apps) with personal settings (Account settings, Keyboard shortcuts, Diagnostics), and the
workspace-name menu only switches workspaces
([settings-menu.png](review-assets/2026-10-05/settings-menu.png)). Follow the convention people
already know:

- **Workspace name menu** — Invite people, People and roles, Apps and integrations, Hosting (on the
  hosting computer), Workspace settings, Switch workspace.
- **Your avatar** — Set a status, Pause notifications, Profile, Preferences, Keyboard shortcuts,
  Help and diagnostics, Sign out.

_Done when:_ every item lives in exactly one of the two, and a five-person test finds Invite and Set a
status first time.

**UX-23 · A calmer conversation header (M).** The header carries Huddle, four unlabeled icons (pins,
saved, scheduled, members) and a search field that duplicates Jump to
([WorkspaceScreen.tsx:950–995](../packages/ui/src/screens/WorkspaceScreen.tsx#L950)). Make it: the name
(opens details), the topic, a stack of member avatars with a count (opens members), **Huddle**, and a
`⋯` menu for pins, notification settings, search in this conversation and details. Show a slim
"1 pinned" bar under the header when there are pins, instead of an icon. _Done when:_ the header has
at most three visible controls besides the name, each with a text label or tooltip.

**UX-24 · Write the container rule (S).** Side panel for things read beside the conversation (thread,
members, pins, Activity, search results); modal for short tasks (create a channel, invite); full page
for configuration (preferences, workspace settings, hosting). Add it to `CONTRIBUTING.md` and move
Friends, Account settings and Manage hosting accordingly (UX-20, UX-60, UX-62). _Done when:_ every
surface matches the rule.

**UX-25 · One place for global status (M).** In this Linux test run, which had no system key store,
the desktop app stacked a title strip, a key-store warning, a "Tandem · Your workspace" strip, the app
and a hosting footer — four horizontal bars of chrome around the conversation. Any warning produces
the same stack. Show connection and hosting state in the sidebar header (where "Connected · Your server"
already is) and give banners a single slot with a priority order, one at a time. _Done when:_ at most
one banner is visible and nothing else steals vertical space from the conversation.

## Phase 3 — Conversation

**UX-30 · Fewer message actions, one menu (M).** Replace the 14-button rail
([MessageItem.tsx:25](../packages/ui/src/components/MessageItem.tsx#L25),
[:363](../packages/ui/src/components/MessageItem.tsx#L363)) with three frequent reactions, the emoji
picker, **Reply in thread**, **Save** and `⋯` — which holds copy link, mark unread, pin, edit, copy
text and delete, grouped into sections. Open the same menu on right-click, which messages do not
support today, and keep `↑` to edit. Delete moves into the menu, away from a hover path. _Done when:_
the hover rail has at most seven controls and the right-click menu matches the touch `⋮` menu.

**UX-31 · Useful conversation beginnings (S–M).** Every channel and DM starts with a 64 px icon, an
eyebrow and a 30 px title — about 200 px — and an empty channel offers nothing to do. Use a compact
intro (name, description, who created it and when) and an empty state with real next steps: **Invite
people to #design**, **Add a topic**, or a prompt to say hello. A DM shows the person's avatar, local
time and status. NN/g's empty-state guidance: say what is happening, teach, and give a direct path.
_Done when:_ an empty channel's first screen contains at least one working action.

**UX-32 · A composer that shows its options (M).** Send later only appears after typing, so it is
found by accident, and the formatting row is always open. Make **Send** a split button with "Send
later" in its chevron — always present, like Slack's — and let the formatting row follow a remembered
toggle (`Aa`) on desktop as it already does on the phone. Keep the Enter hint. _Done when:_ scheduling
is reachable from an empty composer in two clicks and the composer is one row shorter by default.

**UX-33 · One unread language (S).** Unread uses a white edge dot, bold text, a red NEW line, a red
mention badge and an orange count badge in different places (Activity switches between red and orange).
Unread is bold plus the dot; mentions are a count badge in one colour; the NEW line uses the attention
colour. _Done when:_ the same state looks the same in the sidebar, rail, Activity and tabs.

**UX-34 · Threads that feel attached (S).** The **Following** pill reads as a primary action and the
thread composer carries the full toolbar in a narrow panel. Make Following a quiet toggle with a bell
icon, and fold the thread composer's formatting by default. _Done when:_ the thread panel has one
accent element at most.

**UX-35 · Huddle bar weight (S).** **Leave** is a filled alert-red button in a bar that sits between
the timeline and the composer. Use a red outline or icon button and keep the bar to one 48 px row.
_Done when:_ the huddle bar does not draw the eye more than an incoming message.

## Phase 4 — First run

**UX-40 · A front door for each workspace (M).** An invitee opening `http://host:8543/` sees a
marketing split screen ("A place for your people.", a fictional preview), then a form headed **Find
your workspace** with an **All workspaces** link
([first-run-signup.png](review-assets/2026-10-05/first-run-signup.png)). When the page is served by a
workspace, make that workspace the hero: its name and icon, member count, Sign in and Create account,
and — when arriving with an invite — "Maya invited you to Northwind". Move the Tandem story to a small
"Powered by Tandem" footer. Add show-password, and say how to recover a forgotten password (ask an
admin, who can reset it). _Done when:_ an invite link opens on a page whose first heading is the
workspace's name and whose first field is focused.

**UX-41 · Two clear doors in the desktop app (S).** "Host a workspace on this computer" is a link
beneath "Nothing here yet?" ([JoinScreen.tsx:567](../packages/ui/src/screens/JoinScreen.tsx#L567)).
Offer two equal choices: **Join a workspace** (workspaces found on this network, an address, or a
pasted invite link) and **Host a workspace on this computer**. Give the host form a visible label
rather than a placeholder only. _Done when:_ a first-time user reaches hosting in one click from
launch.

**UX-42 · Hosting ends with an invite (M).** After hosting, the owner lands in an empty `#general`.
Make setup three short steps: name the workspace, create your account, **invite your people** (the
local address with Copy, a QR code for phones on the same Wi-Fi, and one sentence about internet
sharing with "Set up later"). Then open `#general` with a welcome message that has two actions. No
tour: NN/g found tutorials do not improve task performance, and contextual help works better. _Done
when:_ a first-time host can send an invite within two minutes of launching, in a moderated test.

**UX-43 · Invites without a generate step (S).** Invite people opens to **Generate invite code**. Create
the link immediately with sensible defaults (seven days, unlimited uses), show options inline, add Copy
and a QR code, and confirm with a toast. Keep the loopback warning — it is good. _Done when:_ opening
Invite people shows a link ready to copy.

**UX-44 · Ask guests to stay at the right moment (S).** Guest access is new and already leads with
**Join as guest** where a host allows it. Keeping the identity as an account is offered from the
settings menu and when a guest tries to attach a file. After a guest's first post, offer **Keep this account** once, in the sidebar, with what
they would keep (name, messages, a place in private conversations). _Done when:_ a guest sees the
offer after posting and never before.

## Phase 5 — Finding things

**UX-50 · One command palette (M–L).** Merge **Jump to** and the header search into one ⌘K / Ctrl K
palette. Input first, with no title bar; recent and unread conversations when empty; people with
presence; channels with member counts; commands ("Create channel", "Invite people", "Set status",
"Switch theme"); and **Search messages for "…"** as the last row, which opens results. No open or close
animation — it is used constantly (Superhuman, Linear, Raycast). _Done when:_ every sidebar
destination and the top ten actions are reachable from the palette, and Enter on an empty query opens
the most recent unread conversation.

**UX-51 · Search results first, filters as chips (M).** With two results the first one starts about
500 px into the search dialog, under the filters, recent searches and a syntax sheet
([search.png](review-assets/2026-10-05/search.png)). Show results in the side panel (or the main
area), filters as chips above them (From, In, Has, Date), turn typed modifiers such as `from:@alice`
into chips, move the syntax sheet behind a `?`, and group results by type (messages, files). _Done
when:_ results start within 150 px of the query and every active filter is one click to remove.

**UX-52 · An Activity worth opening (M).** Each card repeats the conversation and author, labels itself
"Unread" inside the Unread tab, prints a numeric date and offers two underlined links
([activity.png](review-assets/2026-10-05/activity.png)). Use rows grouped by conversation with avatar,
a one-line preview and relative time; the whole row opens the message in place; Mark read and Save on
hover; tabs **Mentions**, **Threads** and **All**; **Mark all read**; live updates (UX-08). Batch,
don't multiply — one "5 new in #design" beats five rows. _Done when:_ ten unread items fit in the panel
at 900 px height.

## Phase 6 — Settings and hosting

**UX-60 · Preferences as a page (M).** Account settings is a 720 px modal with eight sections
([set-status.png](review-assets/2026-10-05/set-status.png)). Make **Preferences** a full-page view
with its own navigation and a search field, and a separate **Workspace settings** for admins. Toggles
save immediately with a quiet "Saved"; only text fields need a Save button. _Done when:_ no settings
section scrolls inside a modal.

**UX-61 · Quick status with an end time (S–M).** "Set a status" opens the whole settings dialog. Open a
small popover from the avatar with the presets that exist today, a custom field, and **Clear after**
(30 minutes, 1 hour, Today, This week, Don't clear) — status expiry is an open gap in the 24
September audit. _Done when:_ setting "In a meeting" for an hour takes two clicks and clears itself.

**UX-62 · A home for hosting (M–L).** Manage hosting is one scrolling modal that mixes status, the data
folder path, backups, start-up, the public link and Cloudflare instructions, and ends with **Stop
hosting** and **Back up now** side by side with equal weight
([manage-hosting.png](review-assets/2026-10-05/manage-hosting.png),
[HostDialog.tsx:1636](../packages/ui/src/components/HostDialog.tsx#L1636)). Make it a page with:

- **Overview** — a live status pill, the address with Copy and QR, people connected, and **Invite
  people** as the primary action.
- **Share on the internet** — plain language first ("Let people outside your Wi-Fi join"), the
  Cloudflare and own-address options second, each with what it needs and what it costs.
- **Backups** — the last backup and whether it is healthy, Back up now, the schedule.
- **Advanced** — start-up behaviour, the data folder, the port.
- **Stop hosting** at the bottom, styled as destructive, with the consequence in its confirmation
  ("3 people will be disconnected").

_Done when:_ the Overview fits without scrolling at 1280×800 and Stop hosting is visually distinct from
every other action.

**UX-63 · Apps that start from outcomes (M).** Apps and integrations opens with a paragraph about
`ts` ids, OAuth, Socket Mode and scopes, and links to `docs/INTEGRATIONS.md` — a repository path an
admin cannot open from the app. Lead with templates ("Post CI results to a channel", "Incoming
webhook", "Slash command"), each producing a ready-to-copy example and a **Send test message** button.
Put the compatibility notes behind "How this differs from Slack", linking to the published document.
_Done when:_ an admin can post a test webhook message without reading the compatibility notes.

## Phase 7 — Phone and tablet

**UX-70 · Visible navigation on phones (M–L).** Add a bottom bar with four destinations — **Home**
(conversations), **DMs**, **Activity**, **You** — inside Material's three-to-five range, each with a
label and unread badge. Keep the drawer for the full channel list and a swipe from the left edge to
open it, so switching stays as fast as it is now. _Done when:_ in a phone test, people find Activity
and their DMs without opening the drawer.

**UX-71 · Keyboard and safe areas (M).** The viewport tag has no `interactive-widget` or
`viewport-fit`, and nothing reads `env(safe-area-inset-*)` or `visualViewport`
([apps/web/index.html](../apps/web/index.html)). Add `interactive-widget=resizes-content` and
`viewport-fit=cover`, pad the header and composer by the safe-area insets, and fall back to
`visualViewport` on iOS Safari, which does not support `interactive-widget`. _Done when:_ on a real
iPhone and Android phone the composer stays above the keyboard and nothing sits under the notch or
home indicator.

**UX-72 · Targets big enough for thumbs (S–M).** The message `⋮`, header icons and reaction pills are
small on touch. Meet WCAG 2.2's 24 px minimum everywhere and aim for 44 px on primary phone controls.
_Done when:_ an automated check finds no touch target under 24 px at 390 px width.

**UX-73 · A long-press sheet for messages (S–M).** The `⋮` menu is already good
([phone.png](review-assets/2026-10-05/phone.png)). Open it as a bottom sheet on long-press as well,
with a quick-reaction row at the top, as people expect from WhatsApp and Slack. _Done when:_ reacting
on a phone takes one long-press and one tap.

**UX-74 · Installable web client (S).** Add a manifest and icons so the browser client can be
installed to a home screen. Background push stays the separate policy decision already recorded in the
research. _Done when:_ Chrome on Android offers Install.

## Phase 8 — Voice and the website

**UX-80 · One voice (S, then ongoing).** Copy is friendly but often long or technical for the person
reading it ("claim code", `xoxb`, "ts is an id rather than a time"). Write a one-page guide: plain and
short; buttons start with a verb; sentence case; no jargon on member surfaces, and jargon on admin
surfaces only behind "Learn more"; errors say what happened and how to fix it (GOV.UK). Review every
string in the first-run path against it. _Done when:_ the first-run path has no term a non-technical
invitee would need to look up.

**UX-81 · A public project website (optional, M).** Tandem's front door today is the GitHub README. If
you want one, build a small static site (GitHub Pages) following the Evil Martians study of 100
developer-tool landing pages — no sales talk, clear and simple: a hero with a real product screenshot
and one sentence ("Team chat you host yourself"), three ways to try it (Windows download, Docker,
Node), feature blocks that match what actually works, a trust block on where data lives, an FAQ and a
final call to action. Generate its screenshots from the seeded e2e run so they never go stale. _Done
when:_ the site's claims link to the validation docs that back them.

## Phase 9 — Validate and measure

**UX-90 · Usability rounds (ongoing).** Five participants per round find most problems; run three
rounds — now, after Phase 2 and after Phase 5 — mixing people who use Slack or Discord daily with
people who don't. Tasks: host a workspace and invite someone (desktop); join from an invite link
(browser and phone); reply in a thread; find the pinned checklist; find last week's message about
the budget; set a status for an hour; start a huddle. Record success, time and a one-question ease
score per task.

**UX-91 · Measure without tracking (S).** Tandem is self-hosted and should stay free of third-party
analytics. Use moderated tests, plus Google's HEART framework as a checklist for what to observe:
task success, time from first launch to the first message exchanged with another person, and errors
per task.

**UX-92 · Gates in CI (M).** Visual baselines (UX-15), axe with contrast on the same screens, the
existing type and radius tests, plus an accent budget (UX-10) and a touch-target check (UX-72).

**UX-93 · A review checklist for UI pull requests (S).** Keyboard path, focus visible, both themes,
390 px, empty, loading and error states, no disabled submit, no Refresh on live data, copy follows the
voice guide. Vercel's Web Interface Guidelines are a good base to adapt.

## What not to do

- **No big-bang redesign.** Ship by phase, behind the layout toggle where habits are involved, and
  keep the old path for one release.
- **No onboarding carousel or product tour.** Make the empty states and the first steps teach.
- **No remote fonts, CDNs or analytics.** A LAN workspace must look and behave the same offline.
- **No removing keyboard paths** while moving things around; every moved item keeps its shortcut.
- **No new features to compensate for clutter.** Most items above remove or move something.

## The first two weeks

1. UX-01, UX-02, UX-05, UX-06 — the defects (a day or two).
2. UX-03 and UX-04 — a calmer first run.
3. UX-07 and UX-08 — forms that explain themselves and panels that are live.
4. UX-14 and UX-15 — primitives and visual baselines, so Phases 1–3 can move quickly without
   regressions.
5. Decide UX-11 (identity) and run the first usability round (UX-90) on the current build, to have a
   baseline before anything larger changes.
